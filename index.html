A concise quote should clearly identify the property type, the transaction type, and the specific work included. It is advisable to inquire whether the fee covers contract review, necessary searches, preparation for settlement, and communication with the other party involved in the transaction. Choosing the Right Legal Help

In New South Wales, property transfers can be managed by either a licensed conveyancer or a solicitor. A licensed conveyancer specialises specifically in property transfers, while a solicitor can also provide advice on broader legal issues that may arise. This distinction is often more significant than general claims about convenience or local expertise. For a standard property purchase or sale, the key consideration is often adherence to process and discipline. You will want assurance of thorough contract review, prompt execution of searches, and efficient settlement management. If the contract presents complex legal questions, engaging a solicitor might be the more appropriate choice. It is important to understand that all legal work and advice are provided by the independent conveyancer or solicitor you engage. Therefore, it is essential to ask who will be directly handling your file, who has the authority to sign off on advice, and how questions will be addressed, particularly when deadlines are approaching. Timing and Contract Types

The source page categorises transactions into buyer, seller, first home buyer, off-the-plan, auction, and commercial matters, which is a logical approach to managing risk. A buyer typically requires contract review, searches, and settlement of their purchase. A seller needs the contract for sale prepared, the necessary disclosure documents attached, and the settlement managed for the sale. The timing of these tasks can change significantly depending on the contract type. Matters involving off-the-plan purchases may include sunset clauses and specific disclosure rules, necessitating a more detailed review and targeted inquiries. Crucially, there is no cooling-off period for properties purchased at auction in NSW, making pre-bid review a critical step rather than a task to be completed after a successful bid. Commercial property transactions often involve leases, Goods and Services Tax (GST), due diligence, and commercial settlement processes, which generally require more detailed information to be provided at the outset when requesting a quote.

Key Timing Points Before Settlement

Settlements are now predominantly conducted online via PEXA, which serves as the digital platform for lodging documents and transferring property ownership. While this indicates the method of settlement, it does not negate the importance of the preceding work. Contract review, conducting necessary searches, calculating stamp duty, and completing signing procedures must all be finalised in a timely manner to ensure a smooth transition. For private treaty residential purchases in NSW, most contracts include a five-business-day cooling-off period, which commences from the exchange of contracts and concludes at 5:00 PM on the fifth business day. It is important to note that this protection does not extend to properties bought at auction. For buyers, understanding this sequence is vital. Inquire about when the contract will be reviewed, the expected timeline for searches, whether any issues need resolution before the contract exchange, and what needs to be prepared before the electronic settlement can be scheduled.

Common questions

How much does conveyancing typically cost in Sydney? Professional fees for a standard Sydney conveyance usually range from a few hundred to around fifteen hundred dollars, with many conveyancers offering fixed-fee quotes. Government charges and disbursements, such as title searches and registration fees, are additional costs that vary by property. Always confirm in writing what is included in the fee before engaging a service.

Do I need a licensed conveyancer or a solicitor in NSW? In NSW, either a licensed conveyancer or a solicitor can handle your property transfer. Licensed conveyancers specialise in property transfers, while solicitors can also offer advice on related legal matters. Both are available, and the choice may depend on the complexity of your transaction.

What is the cooling-off period for property purchases in NSW? Most residential contracts for properties bought by private treaty in NSW have a five-business-day cooling-off period, starting from the contract exchange and ending at 5:00 PM on the fifth business day. This period does not apply to properties purchased at auction.
